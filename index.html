KB RetroTool

AI-assisted cataloguing for the National Library of the Netherlands, built at DEUS.AI.

  1. Problem

    More than 50,000 donated books waited to be catalogued. Each one took a cataloguer 15 to 20 minutes of searching and typing.

  2. Decision

    AI proposes, the cataloguer decides. OCR, ML and LLMs fill the record and score every field; nothing is saved until a person confirms.

  3. Outcome

    40–50% less time per book, and a projected ~18 months gained on the library's 2030 digital heritage target.

Associate Product Manager · Discovery to MVP · 2023–2024

Sancus AI

A RAG product that answers from a company's own documents, with the sources to check it.

  1. Problem

    Company knowledge sat in documents nobody searched well, and early AI chat answered without sources, so nobody could use it for a decision.

  2. Decision

    Fewer connectors, better answers. The MVP took uploads only, moved integrations to the beta, and put the effort into citations and curation.

  3. Outcome

    92.3% of answers rated relevant in MVP benchmarks. Sancus then became the LLM method inside KB RetroTool.

How I work

  1. Evidence before opinion

    I start with the people who do the work: observation, interviews, and the documents a company already has. At the KB, that meant watching cataloguers decide whether two books are the same edition.

    KB RetroTool

  2. Scope is a decision

    A date the team can meet beats a feature list it can't. At Feedzai, a guided wizard moved to the next quarter so the team could commit to three set-up features for Q2.

    Feedzai

  3. Design is my leverage

    Twenty years of design means I can prototype the argument. Something people can click gets a clearer answer than a document. At Shell, stakeholders validated the flows on interactive prototypes.
