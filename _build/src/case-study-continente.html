Context and role

In 2012 only 0.9% of grocery shopping in Portugal happened online. The country was coming out of a deep recession, shoppers were very price-sensitive and moved between supermarkets for promotions, and many didn't trust fresh products they couldn't see or touch. Continente.pt converted 1.2% of visits, and 75% of carts were abandoned at checkout.

I worked across design and growth marketing: research, campaign and page design, and growth experiments with the marketing and e-commerce teams.

  • I ran customer experience research and turned it into insights the team could act on.
  • I designed more than 200 campaigns across more than 50 categories: artwork and landing pages.
  • I put growth tactics in place for acquisition and conversion, and measured them.
  • I worked with cross-functional teams on a major redesign of the website: pages, campaigns and user flows that raised customer satisfaction and increased sales by 13%.

Checkout: three in four carts never paid

75% of carts were abandoned at checkout. We A/B tested versions of the checkout flow and tracked completion, time spent and errors in each.

  • We sawLong forms and unclear product substitutions were where people dropped out. Shoppers didn't know what they would get if an item was out of stock.

    So we changedA simpler checkout in 5 steps, with autofill and a visual preview of substitutions, so the shopper sees and can change the replacement before paying.

  • 25% → 30%checkout completionA 20% relative increase; abandonment fell from 75% to 70%.
  • 22%fewer substitution ticketsCustomer service contacts about replaced products.

Retention: what the first basket tells you

Only 15% of new customers bought a second time. We ran a cohort analysis, grouping new customers by the categories in their first order and following them over time.

  • We sawCustomers whose first order included pantry staples came back three times as often.

    So we changed"Pantry Starter" recommendations for new customers right after their first purchase, steering them to the categories that predict a second one.

  • 28%higher 90-day retentionNew customers.
  • 19%higher customer lifetime valueNew customers.
  • €280Kmonthly revenue from retained customersAttributed to the programme.

Promotions: put offers where people look

Seasonal banners had a 12% click-through rate despite the spend behind them. We used heatmaps and eye-tracking to see where attention actually went.

  • We saw83% of users never looked at the banners; their eyes went to search and the category navigation.

    So we changedPromotions moved next to search and navigation, the zones people already used, instead of competing for attention elsewhere.

  • 47%higher click-throughOn seasonal promotions.
  • €1Mmonthly sales from promotion clicks
  • 19%more uptake of promoted products

Campaign design at a supermarket's pace

Behind the experiments ran a constant stream of campaigns: more than 200 across more than 50 categories, each with its artwork and landing page, often on tight seasonal deadlines. The aim was always the same: make the offer obvious in a second and the next click easy.

Lessons

Watch behaviour before you redesign

Eye-tracking showed that people ignored banners and used search. The fix was moving offers into their path, not making banners louder.

The first basket predicts the second

Cohort analysis turned a vague retention goal into one concrete design move: guide new customers to the categories that bring them back.
