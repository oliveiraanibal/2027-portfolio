Context and role

In 2023 DEUS.AI decided to build its own retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) product: a chat that answers from a company's own files instead of the open web. Sancus was the result. It was then put to work in a client project, as the LLM option of KB RetroTool for the National Library of the Netherlands.

I worked on Sancus as associate product manager, aligning stakeholders on the vision, strategy and roadmap and managing the backlog, and I led the UX and UI design, from discovery to the MVP.

  • I ran the discovery: jobs to be done, a competitor review and the target journey.
  • I mapped outcomes to features and helped prioritise the MVP backlog.
  • I designed the product, from wireframes to high-fidelity screens and prototypes.
  • I contributed to the roadmap and the go-to-market plan.

The problem: answers you can't find, or can't trust

In most companies the answer to a question already exists, in a report, a policy or a wiki page, spread over systems nobody searches well. People ask colleagues, dig through folders, or give up. Early AI chat tools made this faster, but their answers came without sources, so nobody could rely on them for a decision.

The jobs Sancus is hired for

I framed the research as jobs to be done: three for the people asking questions, one for the company buying the tool.

  • UserFinding

    "When I'm looking for specific information, I want to find it quickly and reliably, so I can finish my task."

  • UserUnderstanding

    "When I need to understand a complex topic from internal documents, I want a clear summary with source references, so I can trust it and dig further."

  • UserOnboarding

    "When a new person joins my team, I want them to reach our company knowledge on their own, so they become productive sooner."

  • BuyerUsing what we know

    "When our knowledge base is large and scattered, we want every employee to use it, so we make better decisions."

What research changed

Beyond the jobs, I reviewed competing RAG products for business and mapped a target journey for an analyst preparing figures for a meeting. Four findings shaped the MVP:

  • We sawPeople didn't trust an AI answer they couldn't check.

    So we decidedEvery answer cites its sources, and "Why this answer" opens the exact page it came from.

  • We sawAnswer quality depends on what goes in: untitled, outdated or duplicate files produce weak answers.

    So we decidedCuration is part of the product: titles and descriptions at upload, obsolete files hidden, and a dataset chosen per conversation.

  • We sawCompetitors led with many data connectors; few helped people understand and curate documents.

    So we decidedCompete on trustworthy answers, not on the number of integrations.

  • We sawData privacy was the first question every company asked.

    So we decidedAuthentication and per-company data isolation went into the MVP, not a later phase.

Design decisions

Teach the model in four steps

Most first-time users had never used a chat that answers only from their own files. The empty state is a four-step guide (upload files, start a conversation, choose a dataset, ask something), and each step spotlights the control it refers to.

Trade-off: a guided first run instead of an empty chat box. It costs a few seconds and steers people away from asking before any documents are in.

Ask for context at upload, and say why

When a file is uploaded, Sancus suggests a title and asks for a description, with one line of reasoning: a meaningful title and description improve the answers. Documents missing one keep a visible warning, and outdated files can be flagged as obsolete and hidden from chats.

Trade-off: an extra step in the upload. I made it skippable rather than mandatory, and moved the nudge to the document itself so it isn't forgotten.

Show what is ready to use

Indexing a large PDF takes time, and a document that failed to process gives no answers. The knowledge base lists every file with its status (uploading, processing, error or ready), so people know what Sancus can already draw on.

Make every answer checkable

Answers carry numbered citations inline. "Why this answer" opens a panel listing the source documents and pages, with the passage highlighted. Summarise and Regenerate sit under each answer, with thumbs up and down for feedback on quality.

Trade-off: citations make answers longer and the chat busier. We chose verification over a clean chat look, because an unchecked answer can't go into a report.

MVP scope and roadmap

The biggest scope call was breadth against depth. The MVP took PDF, DOCX and TXT uploads only, and moved connectors such as Confluence and SharePoint to the closed beta. That kept the team on answer quality and curation, where we wanted to stand out.

Lessons

Data quality shows up in the interface

A RAG product is only as good as its documents, so curation (titles, descriptions, obsolete files, datasets) had to be designed as carefully as the chat.

Trust is a feature you can see

Citations and "Why this answer" did more for credibility than any accuracy claim. People believe what they can check.

What's next

Connectors to the tools companies already use, answers that turn into reports, and shared chats, so knowledge found once is found for everyone.
