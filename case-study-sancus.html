Case study · DEUS.AI · 2023–2024

Sancus AI: answers a company can check.

A product that answers questions from a company's own documents and shows where each answer came from. I was its Associate Product Manager and led its design, from discovery to MVP.

Role
Associate Product Manager and UX/UI lead
Team
DEUS.AI product team: stakeholders, engineering and data science
Duration
2023–2024, from discovery to MVP
Outcome
92.3% of answers rated relevant in MVP benchmarks. Sancus then became the LLM method inside KB RetroTool.
01

Context

In 2023 DEUS.AI decided to build its own retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) product: a way for an organisation to ask its documents a question in plain language.

DEUS builds AI products for clients, and Sancus was an internal initiative, so there was no client brief to follow. Someone had to decide who it was for, what the first version would and would not do, and in what order to build it. That was my job as Associate Product Manager, together with the design of the product itself.

The timing mattered. Market estimates at the time valued RAG solutions at about USD 1 billion and growing fast, so a first version had to be small enough to ship.

02

Problem

Company knowledge sat in documents nobody searched well. And the AI chat tools of the day answered without sources, so nobody could use an answer for a decision.

People lost time looking for information, departments worked from different versions of it, and new employees took long to find their way. Discovery turned this into jobs to be done, the things people would hire Sancus for:

  • "When I look for specific information, I want to find it quickly and reliably."
  • "When I read a summary, I want source references, so I can trust it and explore further."
  • "When a new colleague joins, I want them to reach company knowledge without asking around."

The second job became the centre of the product. Finding an answer fast was what every competitor promised. Being able to check it was what made an answer usable at work.

03

My role and what I owned

I held two roles: Associate Product Manager and lead designer. The person deciding what to build was also the person who had done the research and would draw the screens.

  • Vision, strategy and roadmap, agreed with DEUS.AI stakeholders.
  • Prioritisation and the product backlog: what went into the MVP and what waited.
  • Discovery: competitor analysis, jobs to be done, an opportunity solution tree and the target user journey.
  • UX and UI: from wireframes and prototypes to the high-fidelity MVP.

Engineering and data science owned the retrieval pipeline and the model choices. My part was deciding what the product should do with them.

The roadmap I proposed

  1. 1 · MVP

    Core retrieval and answers, document upload (PDF, DOCX, TXT), the chat interface and a basic admin panel.

  2. 2 · Closed beta

    Validation with selected customers, accuracy metrics from real use, and the first integrations: Confluence and SharePoint.

  3. 3 · Public launch

    Enterprise features such as role-based access and audit logs, more connectors, and scaling.

04

Key decisions and trade-offs

Decision 1Fewer connectors, better answers

Competitors led with long lists of data connectors. Few of them helped people trust what came out, or curate what went in. I scoped the MVP to file uploads only and put the effort into answer quality and curation.

Chosen

PDF, DOCX and TXT uploads in the MVP. Confluence and SharePoint moved to the beta.

Rejected

Matching competitors connector for connector. It would have spent the MVP on plumbing, with the same unverifiable answers at the end.

Trade-off

The first users have to upload their documents by hand, and the product looks smaller on a feature comparison.

Decision 2Every answer can be checked

Answers carry numbered citations. "Why this answer" opens the source pages with the passage that was used highlighted, next to Summarise, Regenerate and feedback.

Chosen

Sources inside every answer, one click from the page and passage they came from.

Rejected

A clean, chat-only answer. It demos better, but an answer nobody can check cannot go into a report.

Trade-off

Longer answers and a busier chat screen.

Decision 3Curation is part of the product

Answers are only as good as the documents behind them. So upload suggests a title and asks for a description, and says why. Outdated files can be marked obsolete, and each conversation uses a chosen set of documents.

Chosen

Ask people to describe and maintain their documents, inside the normal flow.

Rejected

Ingesting everything silently and leaving quality to the model. Outdated files would have kept surfacing in answers.

Trade-off

An extra step at upload. It can be skipped, and the reminder stays on the document so it isn't forgotten.

05

Outcome

  • 92.3%

    of generated answers rated relevant in MVP benchmark tests.

  • 0.38 s

    to retrieve from 100 files, per query, in the same benchmark.

  • 25%

    less time to integrate Sancus into a client process, as reported for the MVP.

The strongest result was reuse. Sancus became the LLM method inside KB RetroTool, the cataloguing product for the National Library of the Netherlands.

These numbers come from benchmark tests on the MVP, not from customer usage. They show the product answered well and fast; they do not yet show how people used it.

06

What I'd do next

Run the closed beta, and measure what a benchmark can't.

  • Do people open the sources? If they don't, the citations are decoration and the design needs to change.
  • Does curation happen? Track how many documents get a description and how often obsolete ones are marked.
  • Connectors, in order of demand. Confluence and SharePoint first, chosen by what beta customers actually store there.
  • Access control before enterprise sales. Role-based access and audit logs were on the roadmap for launch, and discovery showed that security and compliance come first for enterprises.
