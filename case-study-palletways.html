Case study · Palletways · Contract, 2024–2026

Palletways: every shipper off the legacy portal, before the switch-off.

Palletways had to retire its 15-year-old shipping portal and APIs by a fixed date. I was the Product Owner on contract for the squad that moved customers across, and stayed 15 months, until the old platform was switched off.

Role
Product Owner for customer migration, one of three squads, reporting to the programme's Head of Product. Design sparring partner for the squad's designer
Team
1 designer, 5 engineers, 1 QA and 1 business analyst, plus the onboarding team and account managers
Duration
15 months: 12 initial, extended by 3
Outcome
97% of active shippers and all shipment volume on the new platform, with legacy switched off four weeks ahead of the deadline.
01

Context

Every shipment starts as a booking. Smaller shippers book, print labels, order collections and track deliveries in a web portal. Larger ones connect their own systems directly, through APIs or EDI file exchange.

The portal and those interfaces ran on a platform built 15 years ago. It had to be retired by a fixed date, it couldn't support the services the business wanted to sell next, and every change took months. A new cloud platform was already being built.

The risk was in the move. A shipper who can't book or print a label on Monday can switch carrier by Tuesday, so customers had to move without losing a day of shipping. The integrated shippers were the smaller group, and they carried more than half of the volume.

02

Problem

The new platform covered core booking only. Dozens of features shippers relied on were missing, and nobody knew which ones mattered or which shippers would be stuck without them.

Constraints

  • A fixed end date

    The legacy platform went away in month 15, whatever the state of the migration.

  • Peak season

    No cutovers from mid-November to the end of December.

  • A dependency

    The platform team owned the new core. My squad owned the migration only.

03

My role and what I owned

I joined a running programme. The platform team had built core booking; my squad owned moving the customers across, and I owned what "done" meant for that.

  • Migration strategy, Product Goal and OKRs, agreed with the Head of Product and the steering committee.
  • The wave plan and the exit criteria for each wave.
  • The Product Backlog for migration tooling and parity gaps, and the dependency on the platform team's backlog.
  • Discovery: legacy usage analytics, and interviews with 14 shippers, 6 account managers and the onboarding team.
  • Measurement and governance: a weekly migration burn-up, a RAID log and the fallback plan.

My design background did part of the discovery. I mapped the move as a service blueprint, from invitation to first booking, across the shipper, the portal, the onboarding team, account managers and support. It showed two teams planning to email the same shippers, and no one owning the first booking after the move.

The squad's designer owned the screens. I ran a weekly critique, reviewed flows before backlog refinement, and we tested the guided migration together with 8 shippers before the pilot.

04

From goal to backlog

Product Goal

Every active shipper books on the new platform, with no loss of volume, before the legacy portal is switched off.

Objective: move every shipper before switch-off, without losing volume

  • Key result 1

    80% of active shippers on the new platform by month 12, and all of them by switch-off.

  • Key result 2

    Fewer than 10 support tickets per 100 migrations from wave 2 onward.

  • Definition of migrated

    A shipper counts as migrated when they book on the new platform, not when they have an account there.

Legacy usage analytics made the triage possible: 31 of 212 features covered 95% of what shippers did. Only the 18 blocking gaps had to be closed before a wave could start.

Wave plan

  • Pilot

    20 shippers

    Hand-held, to prove the guided flow and the data copy.

  • Waves 1 and 2

    Portal shippers

    Guided self-service migration that copies accounts, users, address books and service settings.

  • Wave 3

    API and EDI shippers

    A compatibility layer and a sandbox, so existing API calls and EDI files keep working.

  • Switch-off

    Last movers

    Legacy portal and APIs off, then four weeks of hypercare.

A wave closed on its exit criteria: support tickets, failed bookings and booking volume per shipper. Every shipper could fall back to legacy for 14 days after moving, and account managers called any shipper whose bookings dropped.

Sample backlog item

Keep existing API calls working after migration

As the IT lead at an integrated shipper, I want our existing booking and label API calls to keep working after we move, so that we can migrate without an unplanned development project.

Acceptance criteria

  • Given a legacy SOAP booking request, when it reaches the compatibility layer, then it creates the same shipment on the new platform and returns a response in the legacy format.
  • Given a field the new platform doesn't support, then the request is rejected with a clear error and logged for the migration squad.
  • Sandbox credentials are issued automatically when a shipper is added to wave 3.

Must · 13 points, split into 3 stories · Sprint 11 · Serves key result 1

05

Key decisions and trade-offs

Decision 1Small shippers first, large ones last

Small portal-only shippers carried little revenue risk and taught us the flow. Large integrated shippers wouldn't change anything before peak season anyway.

Chosen

Waves ordered by integration type, volume and risk: portal shippers before the peak freeze, API and EDI shippers after it.

Trade-off

More than half of the volume moved in the last three months before switch-off, which left little slack.

Decision 2Don't rebuild every legacy feature

Feature parity was the default expectation. The usage data said otherwise.

Chosen

Close the 18 blocking gaps before a wave and 21 more for the segments that needed them. Cover 16 with a workaround and training.

Rejected

Full parity. We retired 9 gaps, after talking to the shippers who used them.

Trade-off

A small group of shippers needed a workaround and a call from their account manager.

Decision 3A compatibility layer instead of forced API rewrites

Integrated shippers kept their existing calls. The layer takes a legacy SOAP request, creates the shipment through the new platform's REST API and answers in the legacy format.

Chosen

A temporary compatibility layer, with a sandbox opened to integrated shippers ahead of their cutover.

Rejected

Asking every integrated shipper to rebuild their integration. It would have pushed the migration past the end date.

Trade-off

A temporary layer that the platform team now has to maintain and retire.

Decision 4Pause wave 1 and fix the flow

The first 400 self-service migrations produced 31 support tickets per 100, mostly about label printer set-up and renamed features. We paused invitations for two weeks. I paired with the designer on a set-up checklist with a test label and a guide mapping old terms to new ones, and wrote the content brief so that one email and banner sequence replaced three overlapping ones: what changes, what stays, and the date.

Chosen

Stop the wave, redesign steps 3 and 4 of the journey, then resume.

Trade-off

Two weeks without invitations, on a timeline with a fixed end.

Result

Wave 2 came in at 9 tickets per 100, under the key result of 10.

06

Outcome

  • 97%

    of active shippers on the new platform at switch-off. The last 3%, occasional shippers, now book through customer service.

  • 100%

    of shipment volume on the new platform. Legacy was switched off in month 14, four weeks ahead of the deadline.

  • 31 → 9

    support tickets per 100 migrations, from wave 1 to wave 2. The key result was fewer than 10.

One checkpoint was missed on the way: at month 12, 78% of shippers had moved, against a target of 80%. Small portal shippers moved first, so the share of shippers ran ahead of the share of volume until the integrated shippers cut over after peak season.

The set-up checklist was added to the platform team's design system and is now reused for onboarding new customers.

Close-out. Four weeks of hypercare, with failed bookings and tickets reviewed daily, then back to normal support. The compatibility layer moved into the platform team's backlog, with usage per shipper and a retirement date. The programme closed with a final report and a retrospective with the steering committee.

07

What I'd do next

Run a second, unassisted pilot before the first wave.

The pilot was too well supported to be a fair test. Twenty hand-held shippers didn't show what 400 self-service ones would struggle with.
